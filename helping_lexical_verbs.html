Role-to-Role: who decides what follows

Helpers & Heads

Helping verbs set the form of the next verb. The lexical verb, always last, decides what the clause needs.

When helpers stack, they go in this order

A verb phrase can have a maximum of 5 roles: 4 helpers followed by the lexical verb. The four helper slots, in this order, are:

  1. Modal
  2. Perfect have
  3. Continuous be
  4. Passive be

Filling all four gives The room could have been being cleaned. (5 roles). That is the most a verb phrase can hold, because each slot can be used only once and the slots must come in this order. You can skip any slot you do not need.

In real use the limit is 4 roles, which is 3 helpers plus the lexical verb. Examples:

  • She will have been waiting. (modal + perfect + continuous)
  • The room could have been cleaned. (modal + perfect + passive)

Five-role phrases are grammatical but almost never used, as with been being. In the active voice there is no passive be, so 4 roles is the true maximum.

Each helper picks the form of the next verb, which is why only the first verb in the chain (teal) is finite.

A lexical verb decides the clause pattern

The lexical verb decides what, if anything, must follow it. A = adverbial.

Most adverbials are optional extras: She sang yesterday. You can drop them and the pattern stays the same. A few verbs need one to be complete (She lives in Boston), and then it is part of the pattern.

In a passive sentence the object becomes the subject, so the clause shows None: She kicked the ball → The ball was kicked.

3

Traps: where this goes wrong

Easy mistakes with helpers and lexical verbs.

Modals

A modal is finite but never takes -s, and the verb after it is plain V1.

She cans swims. → She can swim.

Do-support

Does carries the -s, so the lexical verb goes back to V1.

Does she plays? → Does she play?

Be alone

Be can be the lexical verb on its own. It is then finite and gives the pattern C.

She is tired. (finite + lexical, pattern C)

Verbs inside O or C

A to-infinitive or -ing phrase inside O or C is non-finite. It never replaces the finite verb of the clause.

I want to learn. (finite: want)

“Been” and “being”

Both are non-finite: been is V3 of be, being is V4. They can never be the first verb.

She has been waiting.

Well done
