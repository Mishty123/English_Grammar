1

The big map

A verb phrase has 1, 2, 3 or 4 roles, counted from the left. Role 1 is finite, and the last role is always the lexical verb.

Finite verb → tense Non-finite forms → aspect and voice Lexical verb → clause pattern
Rule 1

Role 1 is finite

Role 1 is always the finite verb. It shows tense and agrees with the subject. Every later role is non-finite.

Rule 2

Later roles build aspect & voice

Roles 2, 3 and 4 (be + V4, have + V3, be + V3) decide continuous, perfect or passive.

Rule 3

The last role is the lexical verb

The last role, the lexical verb, decides what comes next: None, an Object or a Complement.

Helping roles add no clause pattern. Only the last role, the lexical verb, decides whether the clause has no object, an object or a complement.

4

Finite or non-finite?

How to find the finite verb of a clause.

Finite verb
  • It is Role 1, the first verb of the verb phrase.
  • It shows tense: plays / played.
  • It agrees with the subject: he plays, they play, I am, you are.
  • It jumps in front of the subject in questions and takes not: Has she gone? She has not gone.
  • One finite verb makes one clause.
Non-finite verb
  • No tense and no agreement.
  • To-infinitive: to learn
  • Bare infinitive (V1): can swim
  • -ing form (V4): is eating, Swimming is fun
  • Past participle (V3): has gone, was eaten

Quick test: turn the sentence into a question or add not. The verb that moves or takes not is the finite verb. With no helper, borrow do: She plays → Does she play? (the lexical verb was finite).

Which finite forms give which tense

TenseFinite forms that show it
PresentV1, V5, am, is, are, has, have, do, does and the modals can, may, shall, must

Futurewill + V1 (in this 12-tense chart, will is the finite verb of the future)

Strictly, will is a modal with a present form, but it is the finite verb that carries the time. That is why the future row starts with it.

6

Aspect and voice

Look at what follows the finite verb.

What follows the finite verbMeaningExample
nothing (or V1 after will)Simple: the action as a wholeShe walks. She will walk.
be + V4Continuous: in progressShe is walking.
have + V3Perfect: finished, linked to another timeShe has walked.
have + been + V4Perfect continuous: going on up to a pointShe has been walking.
be + V3Passive voice: the subject receives the actionThe letter was written.

Passive can be combined with the aspects: has been written (perfect passive), is being written (continuous passive). Future continuous has no natural passive form.

7

Clause patterns: None, O, C

The lexical verb decides what, if anything, must follow it.

PatternVerb typeExampleTip
None (S + V)intransitiveThe baby slept.complete without anything after it
O (S + V + O)transitiveShe kicked the ball.ask what? or whom? after the verb
C (S + V + C)linkingThe soup is hot.C describes or renames the subject
O + OditransitiveHe gave her a book.indirect object, then direct object
O + Ccomplex transitiveThey made him captain.C describes or renames the object

What can fill the O or C slot?

FillerCan be O?Can be C?Example
Noun or pronounyesyesShe kicked the ball. My sister is a nurse.
AdjectivenoyesThe soup is hot.
Non-finite verb phraseyesyesI want to learn. I enjoy reading. Her aim is to win.
Finite clauseyesyesI know that she left. The problem is that he left.

Words like quickly, yesterday and in the park are extras (adjuncts). They are not part of the pattern, so they do not change None, O or C.

In a passive sentence the object becomes the subject, so the clause shows None: She kicked the ball → The ball was kicked.

9

Common traps

Where the finite verb is easy to miss.

Modals

A modal is finite but never takes -s, and the verb after it is plain V1.

She cans swims. → She can swim.

Do-support

Does carries the -s, so the lexical verb goes back to V1.

Does she plays? → Does she play?

Be alone

Be can be the lexical verb on its own. It is then finite and gives the pattern C.

She is tired. (finite + lexical, pattern C)

Verbs inside O or C

A to-infinitive or -ing phrase inside O or C is non-finite. It never replaces the finite verb of the clause.

I want to learn. (finite: want)

Two clauses

Each clause has its own finite verb.

I know that she has left.

“Been” and “being”

Both are non-finite: been is V3 of be, being is V4. They can never be the first verb.

She has been waiting.
