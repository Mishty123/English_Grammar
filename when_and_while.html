When & While

one-liners & phrases

Both words join two actions in time. The difference is how long the action lasts.

Colours whenwhile
Quick check A short, one-time event takes when. A long or ongoing action takes while.
Picture key when = a dot, a moment while = a bar, time stretching

Grammar one-liners

1

When = a point in time or a short event.

When she called, I was asleep.

Remember When = a click 📸. It happens once and it's done.

2

While = a stretch of time or a continuing action.

While I was sleeping, she called.

Remember While sounds like “a while”, which is a long time.

3

Interruption: the long action takes past continuous, the short one takes past simple.

I was cooking when the phone rang.

Remember The bar is the long action. The ⚡ is the interruption. I was cooking (bar) when the phone rang (⚡).

4

Two actions at the same time: while with both continuous, or both simple.

She read while he cooked.

Remember Train tracks 🚆. Two actions run side by side and never cross.

5

When also covers states and ages.

When I was a child, I lived in Pune.

Remember When pins a moment or an age, like a pin on a map 📍

6

For the future, use the present tense after both words, never will.

When I arrive, I'll call you.  (Not: When I will arrive.)

Remember Time words keep will out. Say when I arrive, not when I will arrive.

7

While can mean whereas (contrast).

I like tea, while he likes coffee.

Remember While = a scale weighing two different things.

8

While can mean although (formal).

While I agree, I have doubts.

Remember While = although = a “but” is coming. Listen for it after the comma.

9

Both can be shortened with -ing.

While walking, I saw him. When leaving, lock the door.

Remember Cut the “I was” and keep the -ing. The short form is while walking.

10

When can start a question. While cannot.

When does it start?

Remember When is in the WH gang of question words. While only wears the same hat.
