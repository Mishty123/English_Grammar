The verb “to be”

The forms of “be”

“Be” is an irregular verb. Its form changes with the tense and with the subject.

What “be” means as a main verb

As a main verb, “be” is a linking verb. It links the subject to information about it.

There are five kinds of information: exists, happens, located, identity and description.

A. Exists

Says that someone or something is there. Use “there is” for one and “there are” for more than one.

  • There is a rabbit inside.
  • There is nothing in the fridge.
  • There are two cats here.

B. Happens

Says when an event takes place.

  • The party is tonight.
  • The meeting is at five o’clock.
  • The test was yesterday.

C. Located

Says where a person or thing is.

  • She is at school.
  • She is home.
  • The food is on the table.
  • The meeting is down the hall.

D. Identity

Gives a name, a job, a role, or where someone is from (origin).

  • She is Lara and this is Bob.
  • He is a singer.
  • He is not a singer.
  • Alex is from Ireland.
  • Sonia is Spanish.

E. Description

Tells what someone or something is like: colour, age, feelings, weather, price.

  • Our dog is black.
  • I am tired.
  • My father is 50.
  • It is sunny today.

Only pronouns change the verb. Any noun counts as he / she / it (one) or they (more than one). After “will”, the verb is always “be”, for every subject.

Being (V4) shows behaviour and is used only with words like rude or polite. You cannot say “I am being here”. Been (V3) comes after have / has / had and joins the past to now. It does not mean “finished”.

Sometimes “be” is not the main verb. It comes before another verb and helps it. To tell the difference, look at the word after “be”.

Quick pattern to remember
Simple→ BE + V3
Continuous→ BE + BEING + V3
Perfect→ HAVE + BEEN + V3
Future→ WILL + BE + V3

V1 = write  ·  V2 = wrote  ·  V3 = written

Special structures

A. Imaginary situations: “were”

For wishes and things that are not real, use “were” with every subject, even I, he, she and it.

  • If I were you, I would take that deal.
  • He acts as if he were the boss.

In everyday talk, “If I was…” is common. In careful writing, use “were”.

B. Plans and orders: be + to + verb

Be + “to” + V1 shows a fixed plan or an order.

Subject + am / is / are + to + V1

  • Plan: The Prime Minister is to visit France tomorrow.
  • Order: You are to finish your homework before watching TV.

Ways “be” is used

From simple to advanced. Each way shows the pattern first, then examples.

Level 1 · Basic

Be after a modal verb

After will, can, might, must, should

After a modal verb, the verb is always “be”. It never changes.

Subject + modal + be + …

  • Dinner will be ready soon.
  • The kids might be at the park.
  • You must be careful.

Be as a link verb

Level 2 · Intermediate

Be as a helping verb

Be + V4: continuous

Shows an action in progress. See Helping verb 1.

Subject + be form + V4

  • We were walking home.
  • It has been raining all day.

Be + V3: passive

The subject receives the action. See Helping verb 2.

Subject + be form + V3

  • The window was broken.
  • These toys are made of wood.
Level 3 · Advanced

Be after a noun, or after “it”

Noun + be + that-clause

Names a problem, an idea or a fact, then explains it with a clause.

Noun + be form + that + clause

  • The problem is that I lost my pencil.
  • The good thing is that nobody was hurt.

Common nouns: problem, idea, fact, hope, reason, rule, truth, trouble.

Noun + be + to + V1

Names a plan or a wish, then says what it is.

Noun + be form + to + V1

  • My plan is to read every night.
  • Her wish was to see the sea.

Common nouns: plan, aim, wish, hope, idea, job, promise, decision.

It + be + adjective + clause

Comments on something that happened or is true. The clause starts with that or a question word (what, who, how).

It + be form + adjective + that / wh- clause

  • It is lucky that we found the bag.
  • It is not clear who won.
  • It was amazing how fast she ran.

Common adjectives: clear, true, lucky, strange, sad, odd, possible, obvious.
