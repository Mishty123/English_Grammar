The Building Blocks of Sentences

Clauses

On this page

    A clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb. It can stand alone as a sentence, or be part of a larger one.

    Quick check: does it have a subject, and does it have a verb? If yes to both, it's a clause.

    Two main types

    Independent Clause stands alone

    A clause that can stand alone as a complete sentence. It has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought.

    • She loves reading.
    • The sun is shining.
    • They went to the park.

    Dependent Clause cannot stand alone — also called a subordinate clause

    A clause that cannot stand alone as a sentence. It begins with a subordinating conjunction and depends on an independent clause to complete its meaning.

    • Because it was raining.
    • When I finish my homework.
    • Although she was tired.

    Combining clauses

    These are the three main ways to connect two related sentences into one.

    1. Using subordinating conjunctions

    Join a dependent clause to an independent clause.

    Dependent: Because it was raining
    Independent: We stayed home.
    Combined: Because it was raining, we stayed home.

    2. Using relative pronouns

    Join an adjective clause to a noun.

    The boy broke the window. The boy apologized.
    Combined: The boy who broke the window apologized.

    3. Using semicolons or connectors

    For joining two independent clauses without a subordinating conjunction.

    I wanted to go out; it was raining.
    I wanted to go out; however, it was raining.

    Note: common connectors: however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, consequently, meanwhile

    Common mistakes

    ✗Because that is not my pen. (Incomplete — a dependent clause left dangling on its own) ✓That is not my pen. (Complete — an independent clause)
    ✗Because he was tired. (Incomplete) ✓Because he was tired, he went to bed. (Complete)
    ✗She left early which I was surprised. (Incorrect — “which” needs a noun or clause to modify) ✓She left early, which surprised me. (Correct)

    Remember

    • A clause must have a subject and a verb — that's what separates it from a phrase.
    • Independent clauses can stand alone; dependent clauses need an independent clause to complete their meaning.
    • Subordinating conjunctions signal the relationship between the two ideas — time, reason, contrast, condition, place, or manner.
    • Practice combining clauses for stronger, more varied sentences.
