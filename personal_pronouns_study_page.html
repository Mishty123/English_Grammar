Personal Pronouns

A personal pronoun takes the place of a noun that names a person or thing. Its form changes with person, number, gender and its job in the sentence.

The four things a pronoun shows

Person

1st person is the speaker (I, we). 2nd person is the listener (you). 3rd person is anyone or anything else (he, she, it, they).

Number

Singular means one. Plural means more than one.

Gender

Only 3rd person singular shows gender: he (male), she (female), it (thing, animal, or a situation).

Job (case)

The job decides the form: subject, object, or ownership. The rest of this page is about jobs.

  • it has no possessive pronoun. its is only a possessive adjective.

Subject pronouns I, you, he, she, it, we, they

Use a subject pronoun when it does the action of the verb, or takes the place of the subject.

Subject of a verb

She sings. They play.

Find the verb. Ask who or what does it.

Compound subject

My brother and I went home.

Test: remove the other person. You say “I went home,” so use I.

Subject in a question

Is she here? Can we go?

In a question the subject comes after the helping verb. The form stays the subject form.

After than or as

My sister is older than I (am).

She likes him more than I (like him).

She likes him more than me (than she likes me).

Add the missing verb. If the pronoun would be the subject of that verb, use the subject form. If it would be the object, use the object form. The two forms give different meanings.

After the verb be

“Who is it?” “It is I.”

Formal grammar and exams use the subject form. In everyday speech, “It’s me” is normal and accepted.

Object pronouns me, you, him, her, it, us, them

Use an object pronoun for the receiver of the action, or after a preposition.

Direct object

She called me. He saw them.

The verb is transitive. Ask “whom?” or “what?” after the verb.

Indirect object

Dad gave her a toy.

= Dad gave a toy to her.

The person who receives the thing that is given.

After a preposition

with him, for us, to them, about it

Everyone came except him. Between you and me, I agree.

Every preposition takes the object form, including except, but (meaning except), like and between.

Compound object

He invited Sara and me.

Test: remove the other person. You say “He invited me.”

Before a base verb or to + verb

Let me help. Make them stop. She told us to wait.

After let, make, help and verbs like tell, ask, want, the pronoun is an object. Let’s means let us, so do not write “Let’s us go.”

Short answers (informal)

“Who wants tea?” “Me!”

Spoken English uses the object form alone. A full formal answer is “I do.”

Possessive adjectives my, your, his, her, its, our, their

They show who owns the noun that comes next, so they never stand alone.

Always before a noun

my pen, your bag, our house

A possessive adjective is always followed by a noun.

Match the owner

Sara loves her brother.

Tom loves his sister.

Choose his or her by the owner (Tom, Sara), not by the thing owned (sister, brother).

No change for plural nouns

my book, my books

Only the owner changes the word.

Body parts and clothes

He washed his hands. She put on her coat.

English uses a possessive here, not the.

No second determiner

my book. Not “the my book” or “a my book.”

To say “a” with a possessive, use a possessive pronoun: a friend of mine.

Before an -ing word

I don’t mind your waiting.

Formal style uses the possessive adjective. Everyday speech often says “you waiting.”

Look-alike words

The dog wagged its tail. It’s raining.

their house, over there, they’re late

its belongs to it. it’s is it is or it has. their belongs to them. there is a place. they’re is they are.

Possessive pronouns mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs

They stand alone in place of a possessive adjective and its noun. it has no possessive pronoun.

As the subject

Mine is red. Yours is on the table.

After be

That bag is hers.

As an object

I like yours. He took ours.

After than or as

Their house is bigger than ours.

Double possessive

a friend of mine, a cousin of hers

Not “a friend of me.”

No apostrophe

yours, hers, ours, theirs

Never your’s, her’s, our’s or their’s. his stays his.

Reflexive and intensive pronouns myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, oneself, ourselves, yourselves, themself, themselves

They end in -self or -selves. The same words do two jobs.

Reflexive: the doer and the receiver are the same person

Direct object

I cut myself. She looked at herself.

Indirect object

She bought herself a coat.

“She bought her a coat” means a different woman.

After a preposition

He talked to himself. I am proud of myself.

She did it by herself.

by + reflexive means alone, or without help.

Fixed phrases

Enjoy yourself. Help yourselves. Behave yourself.

Verbs that often drop it

He washed and dressed. She shaved.

With wash, dress, shave, relax and similar verbs, English leaves out the reflexive when the meaning is clear. Keep it for emphasis: The child can dress herself.

Intensive: adds emphasis

After a noun or pronoun

The president himself opened the door.

I myself checked the report.

At the end

I did it myself.

Test. Delete the -self word. If the sentence still works with the same meaning, it is intensive. If the sentence breaks or changes meaning, it is reflexive.

  • A reflexive pronoun is never a subject. Write “My wife and I went,” not “My wife and myself went.”
  • Use it only when it is the same person as the subject. Write “Please contact me,” not “Please contact myself.”
  • Say himself and themselves. “Hisself” and “theirselves” are not standard.

You, it, they and others

A few pronouns have uses that surprise learners.

you

You are late. (one person or many)

It is singular and plural, but the verb is always the plural form: you are, you were, you have, you go. Never “you is.”

it for things

I have a book. It is new.

Use it for things, for animals, and for a baby when the sex is not known or not important.

it as an empty subject

It is raining. It is 5 o’clock. It is far from here.

Here it has no meaning of its own. The sentence needs a subject, so it fills the place. Used for weather, time and distance.

it holding the place of a later subject

It is important to study.

It was Sara who called.

The real subject (to study) comes later. In the second sentence, It was ... who puts the focus on Sara.

he and she for animals

My dog is old. She sleeps all day.

People use he or she for a pet or animal they know well. Use it when the animal is just an animal.

they for one person

Someone called. They left a message.

Modern English uses they for one person whose gender is unknown, not stated, or who uses they. The verb stays plural: they are, they were. Older exam style writes “he or she.”

you for people in general

You never know what will happen.

Here you does not mean the listener. It means anyone, people in general. It is informal. Formal writing uses one: “One never knows.”

they with no noun before it

They say it will snow tomorrow. They closed the road.

No noun is named. they means an unnamed group of people, often the authorities. The verb is plural.

Sentence rules

Rules about where a pronoun goes and which small words go with it.

Never drop the subject

Wrong: Is raining. Am very tired.

Right: It is raining. I am very tired.

English needs a subject before every verb. The only exception is a command, where you is understood: “Close the door.”

Do not double the subject

Wrong: My brother he is tall.

Right: My brother is tall.

Wrong: The book that I bought it was new. Right: The book that I bought was new.

The noun is already the subject. A pronoun after it repeats the subject. In the second pair, that already stands for the book, so it repeats the object.

Pronoun + noun

We students need help.

She told us students to wait.

The pronoun takes the form of its job. Test: delete the noun. “We need help.” “She told us to wait.”

both, all, each + of

both of us · all of them · each of you

After of, use the object form. “Both of we” is wrong.

we both, they all

We both like tea. They all came.

When both or all comes right after the subject pronoun, the subject form stays. We both means both of us.

Capital I

Tom and I went. Can I come?

I is always a capital letter, in any position. Every other pronoun is lowercase unless it starts a sentence.

own

my own room · a room of my own

own follows a possessive adjective and adds emphasis: the room belongs to me alone. Wrong: “an own room.”

Agreement

A pronoun must match the noun it replaces, which is called its antecedent. The verb must match the pronoun.

Pronoun and antecedent

Number, gender, person

The boy lost his bag. The boys lost their bags.

One pronoun, one noun

Unclear: Maria told Anna that she was late.

Clear: Maria told Anna, “You are late.”

If a pronoun could mean two nouns, repeat the noun or rewrite the sentence.

everyone, someone, each, nobody

Everyone should bring their book.

These words are singular and take a singular verb (everyone is). Modern English uses their after them. Older exam style writes his or her.

team, family, class, committee

The team lost its game. (one unit)

The team put on their coats. (members, each acting)

A group acting as one unit takes it / its. Members acting separately take they / their. British English often treats the group as plural: “The team have lost their game.”

Two subjects joined together: the verb and the pronoun

V1 is the base verb (go, have). V5 is V1 + s/es (goes, has). be forms: I am, you / we / they are, he / she / it is. Past: was / were.

Joined byThe verb matchesVerb examplePronoun example
andBoth together, so plural: V1, are / were / have. Even when both are singular.He and I go. You and I are friends. She and he were late.Tom and Mary lost their keys.

The same rules work for nouns: Tom and Mary take V1. Tom or Mary takes V5.

Order in a compound

Put yourself last. Say “Tom and I went,” “He gave it to Mary and me,” and “you and I.”

Which form? Four questions

Ask them in order. Stop at the first yes.

  1. Is it the same person as the subject, and the receiver of the action? Use the reflexive form. (I cut myself.)
  2. Does it own a noun that follows? Use the possessive adjective. If nothing follows, use the possessive pronoun. (my pen / mine)
  3. Is it the doer of a verb, including a verb you can add after than or as? Use the subject form.
  4. Is it the receiver of a verb, or after a preposition? Use the object form.

Practice

Answers are hidden. Try each question first, then tap Answer. Every use case above appears at least once.

Part A Subject pronouns

  1. (She / Her) and (I / me) are best friends.
    Answer

    She and I. Compound subject. Test: “She is a friend” and “I am a friend.”

  2. My sister is older than . (I / me)
    Answer

    I. Add the missing verb: older than I am. Spoken English accepts me, but use I in exams.

  3. Meaning: Sara likes Tom more than I like Tom. Complete: Sara likes Tom more than . (I / me)
    Answer

    I. The missing verb is like him, and I is its subject. Writing me would mean “more than she likes me.”

  4. is raining today. (It / He)
    Answer

    It. Empty subject for weather.

  5. Are ready? (they / them)
    Answer

    they. In a question the subject comes after the helping verb. The form stays the subject form.

  6. “Who is it?” “It is .” Choose the formal answer. (I / me)
    Answer

    I. After be, formal grammar uses the subject form. In everyday speech, “It’s me” is accepted.

  7. You late. (is / are)
    Answer

    are. you always takes are, for one person or many.

Part B Object pronouns

  1. The teacher called . (I / me)
    Answer

    me. Direct object of the transitive verb called.

  2. Dad gave a toy. (she / her)
    Answer

    her. Indirect object. It equals “gave a toy to her.”

  3. This gift is for . (we / us)
    Answer

    us. Object of the preposition for.

  4. Everyone came except . (he / him)
    Answer

    him. except is a preposition.

  5. The teacher invited Sam and . (I / me)
    Answer

    me. Compound object. Test: “The teacher invited me.”

  6. Between you and , I don’t like it. (I / me)
    Answer

    me. between is a preposition.

  7. Let help you. (I / me)
    Answer

    me. After let, the pronoun is an object.

  8. She told to wait. (we / us)
    Answer

    us. Object before to + verb.

  9. “Who wants tea?” “!” Choose the informal short answer. (I / Me)
    Answer

    Me. Spoken short answers use the object form. A full formal answer is “I do.”

Part C Possessive adjectives and possessive pronouns

  1. This is pen. (my / mine)
    Answer

    my. A noun follows, so use the possessive adjective.

  2. This pen is . (my / mine)
    Answer

    mine. Nothing follows, so use the possessive pronoun.

  3. Sara loves brother. (his / her)
    Answer

    her. The owner is Sara.

  4. Tom loves sister. (his / her)
    Answer

    his. Match the owner, Tom, not the sister.

  5. The dog wagged tail. (it’s / its)
    Answer

    its. It shows ownership. it’s means it is.

  6. a sunny day. (It’s / Its)
    Answer

    It’s. It is a sunny day.

  7. He brushed teeth. (him / his)
    Answer

    his. English uses a possessive with body parts.

  8. A friend of called me. (me / mine)
    Answer

    mine. Double possessive: of + possessive pronoun.

  9. Is this book ? (your’s / yours)
    Answer

    yours. No apostrophe.

  10. Which bag is ? (their / theirs)
    Answer

    theirs. It stands alone.

  11. Their house is bigger than . (our / ours)
    Answer

    ours. After than, with no noun following.

Part D Reflexive and intensive pronouns

  1. I cut while cooking. (me / myself)
    Answer

    myself. The doer and the receiver are the same person.

  2. She looked at in the mirror. (her / herself)
    Answer

    herself. Reflexive, after a preposition.

  3. He bought a watch. He bought it for his own self. (him / himself)
    Answer

    himself. Reflexive indirect object. “He bought him a watch” means a different man.

  4. We enjoyed at the party. (us / ourselves)
    Answer

    ourselves. enjoy + reflexive.

  5. She did it by . (her / herself)
    Answer

    herself. by herself means alone, or without help.

  6. Please send it to . (me / myself)
    Answer

    me. The subject is you, a different person, so the reflexive is wrong.

  7. My wife and went. (I / myself)
    Answer

    I. A reflexive is never a subject.

  8. They built the house . (theirselves / themselves)
    Answer

    themselves. Intensive. “Theirselves” is not standard.

  9. Reflexive or intensive? “She herself fixed the car.”
    Answer

    Intensive. Delete herself and the sentence still works: “She fixed the car.”

  10. The president opened the door. (himself / him)
    Answer

    himself. Intensive, for emphasis.

Part E Agreement

  1. Tom and Mary lost keys. (his / her / their)
    Answer

    their. Two people joined by and are plural.

  2. The girls forgot lunches. (her / their)
    Answer

    their. girls is plural.

  3. Neither the boys nor Mary finished work. (her / their)
    Answer

    her. With nor, match the nearer noun, Mary.

  4. Which sentence is clear? (a) Maria told Anna that she was late. (b) Maria told Anna, “You are late.”
    Answer

    (b). In (a), she could mean Maria or Anna.

  5. She and he here. (is / are)
    Answer

    are. and makes the subject plural, even when both pronouns are singular.

  6. Tom and I a car. (has / have)
    Answer

    have. Plural subject, V1.

  7. Either he or they to school. (go / goes)
    Answer

    go. The nearer subject is they, so V1.

  8. Either they or he to school. (go / goes)
    Answer

    goes. The nearer subject is he, so V5.

  9. Neither she nor I late. (is / am)
    Answer

    am. The nearer subject is I.

  10. He, along with me, to school. (go / goes)
    Answer

    goes. along with does not join the subjects. The verb matches the first subject, he.

  11. (Tom and I / I and Tom) went to the park.
    Answer

    Tom and I. Put yourself last.

  12. Someone called. (He / They) left a message. Choose the modern form for a person whose gender is not known.
    Answer

    They. Modern English uses they for one unknown person. The verb stays plural.

  13. Everyone should bring book. Choose the modern form. (his or her / their)
    Answer

    their. everyone is singular, but modern English uses their after it. his or her is older exam style.

  14. The team lost game. The team acted as one unit. (its / their)
    Answer

    its. A group acting as one unit takes it / its.

  15. The team put on coats. Each member put on a coat. (its / their)
    Answer

    their. The members act separately, so use they / their.

Part F Fix the sentence

  1. Me and him went to the market.
    Answer

    He and I went to the market. Compound subject, subject forms, yourself last.

  2. Give it to she and I.
    Answer

    Give it to her and me. Object forms after to.

  3. Myself and Tom made the cake.
    Answer

    Tom and I made the cake.

  4. That book is your’s.
    Answer

    That book is yours.

  5. Let’s us go.
    Answer

    Let’s go. or Let us go.

  6. He hurt hisself.
    Answer

    He hurt himself.

  7. Its raining heavily.
    Answer

    It’s raining heavily.

  8. Please contact myself.
    Answer

    Please contact me.

Part G Special uses and sentence rules

  1. In “You never know,” does you mean the listener or people in general?
    Answer

    People in general. This is the generic you. Formal writing uses one.

  2. “They say it will snow.” Who is they?
    Answer

    An unnamed group. No noun comes before it. The verb is plural.

  3. Fix: Is raining.
    Answer

    It is raining. Every verb needs a subject.

  4. Fix: Am very tired.
    Answer

    I am very tired.

  5. Fix: My brother he is tall.
    Answer

    My brother is tall. The noun is already the subject.

  6. Fix: The book that I bought it was new.
    Answer

    The book that I bought was new. that already stands for the book.

  7. students need help. (We / Us)
    Answer

    We. Delete the noun: “We need help.”

  8. The teacher told students to wait. (we / us)
    Answer

    us. Delete the noun: “The teacher told us to wait.”

  9. Both of like tea. (we / us)
    Answer

    us. After of, use the object form.

  10. All of came. (they / them)
    Answer

    them. Object form after of.

  11. (We both / Both we) like tea.
    Answer

    We both. It means both of us.

  12. Fix: Tom and i went home.
    Answer

    Tom and I went home. I is always a capital letter.

  13. Fix: I want an own room.
    Answer

    I want my own room. or I want a room of my own.
