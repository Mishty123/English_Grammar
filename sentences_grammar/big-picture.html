    The Big Picture: How It All Fits Together

    Every other grammar topic you'll meet belongs to one of two layers on top of the basic patterns in Part 2 of the Sentence Patterns tab. Layer A fills a slot inside a pattern. Layer B is a transformation applied to a whole clause, independent of which pattern it uses.

    Layer A — Slot-Fillers (fill the S / V / O-C / A slots)

    A slot in a basic pattern doesn't have to be a single word — it can be filled by a phrase or by an entire dependent clause. This is how simple 5- or 7-word patterns grow into long, complex sentences.

    A1 — Phrases (10 types) — typically fill a single slot

    Phrase TypeTypically Fills
    Noun PhraseSubject, Object, or Complement slot
    Verb PhraseThe Verb slot (main verb + helping verbs)
    Adjective PhraseModifies a noun inside the S, O, or C slot
    Adverb PhraseThe Adverbial slot, or modifies the verb
    Prepositional PhraseAdverbial slot (or modifies a noun within S/O/C)
    Gerund PhraseSubject or Object slot (acts as a noun)
    Participial PhraseModifies a noun inside the S or O slot
    Infinitive PhraseSubject, Object, Complement, or Adverbial slot
    Appositive PhraseRenames a noun inside the S or O slot
    Absolute PhraseAttaches to the whole clause, not one slot

    A2 — Dependent Clauses (11 types) — fill a slot with a full clause

    Of the 11 traditionally-listed dependent clause types, only two are structurally distinct at the slot level: the Noun Clause and the Adjective (Relative) Clause. The other 9 are all the Adverb Clause, subdivided by meaning — every one of them fills the same Adverbial slot.

    Clause TypeTypically Fills
    Noun ClauseSubject, Object, or Complement slot
    Adjective (Relative) ClauseModifies a noun inside the S or O slot
    Adverb Clause (general)Adverbial slot
      ↳ Conditional — states a condition (if/unless…)
      ↳ Temporal (Time) — states when
      ↳ Cause — states why
      ↳ Concessive — states an unexpected contrast
      ↳ Place — states where
      ↳ Manner — states how
      ↳ Purpose — states the intention behind the action
      ↳ Result — states the outcome of a degree

    A3 — Minor Fixed Structures — specific phrase/clause shapes that also just fill a slot

    StructureTypically Fills
    Degrees of Comparison (comparative/superlative)Modifies a noun in the S, O, or C slot — or modifies a verb via the Adverbial slot
    Comparative Correlatives (“the more…, the better…”)Pairs two whole clauses, like a conditional — not a single slot
    So…that / Such…that (result)Adverbial slot — states a result
    Too…to / Enough…toAdverbial slot, or the Complement slot when modifying an adjective/adverb
    Purpose Clauses (in order to / so that)Adverbial slot — states intention (same as the Purpose Clause in A2)
    Concessive Clauses (even though / despite)Adverbial slot — unexpected contrast (same as the Concessive Clause in A2)
    Causative Constructions (have/get/make/let + object + verb)Expands the Verb slot into Verb + Object + Object Complement
    Preference Structures (would rather / would prefer)Expands the Verb slot into Verb + Object (bare infinitive or that-clause)
    Gerund vs. Infinitive (verb complementation)Object slot — the gerund/infinitive phrase acts as the direct object
    Embedded / Indirect QuestionsSubject, Object, or Complement slot — functions as a Noun Clause
    Question TagsAttaches to the whole sentence — doesn't fill a slot
    Cleft Sentences (“It was John who…”, “What I need is…”)Restructures the sentence to spotlight whichever slot (S, O, or A) is being emphasized

    Layer B — Fundamental Grammar Dimensions (whole-clause transformations)

    These five dimensions are different: they don't fill a slot. Each one takes an entire clause — already built from a basic pattern — and changes its form (or pairs it with another clause) without changing which pattern it's built on.

    DimensionWhat it does to the clause
    Voice (Active & Passive)Swaps which slot the doer vs. the receiver of the action occupies, across simple, progressive, perfect, and perfect continuous forms.
    Mood (Indicative, Imperative, Subjunctive)Changes the verb form to mark a statement, a command, or a hypothetical/unreal situation.
    Conditionals (Zero to Third, Mixed)Pairs an if-clause with a result clause under a shared tense-logic system, from general truths to unreal past.
    Word Order — InversionReorders subject and auxiliary for questions, negative-adverb fronting, or formal conditional style.
    Reported SpeechWraps a clause inside a reporting clause and back-shifts its tense and time/place references.

    The full picture, in one line

    A sentence = one or more basic patterns (Part 2), each slot optionally filled by a phrase or dependent clause (Layer A) instead of a single word, with the whole clause optionally reshaped by voice, mood, a conditional pairing, inversion, or reported speech (Layer B) — and the whole thing carries a purpose (Part 3) and a structure (Part 4).
