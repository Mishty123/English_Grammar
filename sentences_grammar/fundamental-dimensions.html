English Grammar Reference

Fundamental Dimensions

Five major systems that govern how a clause's meaning is built. Every clause you write sits somewhere on each of these five scales at once.

1 · Fundamental Dimension

Voice — Active & Passive

Whether the subject performs the action (active) or receives it (passive), across all four aspects.

AspectActivePassive
SimpleSubject + verbSubject + is/was + V3
ProgressiveSubject + is/was + V-ingSubject + is/was + being + V3
PerfectSubject + has/had + V3Subject + has/had + been + V3
Perfect ContinuousSubject + has/had + been + V-ingrarely used in modern English
  • Mario tests his friends. → His friends are tested (by Mario).
  • Mario is testing his friends. → His friends are being tested.
  • Mario has tested his friends. → His friends have been tested.
Rule to know: Passive voice shifts focus away from the doer. Only include “by + agent” when the doer genuinely matters to the meaning.

2 · Fundamental Dimension

Mood — Indicative, Imperative, Subjunctive

Whether a verb states fact, gives a command, or expresses a wish/demand/hypothetical that isn't yet real.

MoodForm
Indicativenormal tense forms for statements and questions
Imperativebase verb, subject dropped
Subjunctivefixed base-verb form, unaffected by subject or tense back-shift
  • He experienced the magic. (indicative)
  • Take it. (imperative)
  • It's essential that he sit still. / I wish he were here. (subjunctive)
Rule to know: The subjunctive never inflects for person or number, and cannot take progressive or perfect-continuous forms — “he sit” stays “sit” no matter the subject.

3 · Fundamental Dimension

Conditionals — Zero to Third, and Mixed

If-clause and result-clause pairings that express general truths, real possibilities, or unreal/hypothetical situations.

TypeStructure
Zero (general truth)If + present simple, present simple
First (real future)If + present simple, will + base verb
Second (unreal present)If + past simple, would + base verb
Third (unreal past)If + past perfect, would have + V3
MixedIf + past perfect, would + base verb
  • If it rains, the ground gets wet. (zero)
  • If he tries again, he will succeed. (first)
  • If he had tested them sooner, he would know now. (mixed)
Rule to know: Never put “will” or “would” inside the if-clause itself — they belong only in the result clause.

4 · Fundamental Dimension

Word Order — Inversion

A fixed subject–auxiliary swap triggered by questions, negative-adverb fronting, or formal conditional structures.

TriggerStructure
QuestionsAuxiliary + subject + verb
Negative frontingNever/Rarely/Not only + auxiliary + subject + verb
Conditional inversionHad/Were/Should + subject + … (“if” dropped)
  • Did he fall?
  • Never had he seen such a chair.
  • Had he tried again, he would have succeeded.
Rule to know: Only the auxiliary or modal verb inverts with the subject — a main verb alone never moves in front of its subject.

5 · Fundamental Dimension

Reported Speech — Statements, Questions, Commands

Relays someone's original words indirectly, shifting tense, pronouns, and time/place references one step back.

TypeStructure
Statementsreporting verb + that + back-shifted clause
Questionsreporting verb + if/whether or wh-word + subject + verb (no inversion)
Commandsreporting verb + object + to-infinitive
  • “I am happy,” he said. → He said that he was happy.
  • “Are you ready?” she asked. → She asked if he was ready.
  • “Wait here,” he told Mario. → He told Mario to wait there.
Rule to know: Back-shift the tense one step into the past, and update time/place words (today → that day, here → there) to match the new speaker's perspective.
