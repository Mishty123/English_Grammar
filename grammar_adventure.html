Remember: an adjective goes before the noun (a red car) or after is / feels / seems (the car is red).
It never adds "s": two big dogs ✅ (not "bigs").

🔒 Grown-up note (tap to open)

After is / feels / seems, the adjective is a subject complement (also called a predicative adjective): it describes the subject, not the verb. These are linking (copular) verbs: be (am, is, are, was, were) and others such as seem, become, look, feel, sound, smell, taste, get, grow, stay, remain.

Many linking verbs are state verbs (seem, feel, look), but the two groups are not the same. Some state verbs do not link (know, own, love), and some linking verbs show change, not a state (become, get, turn). Also, feel / look / smell / taste can be action verbs with no complement: She looked at me.

Degrees of Comparison: 🐶 small → 🐱 smaller → 🐭 smallest (tall → taller → tallest)

👉
No longer adjectives! Words that answer "Which one?", "Whose?" and "How many?" (like this, my, many) used to be called adjectives in old grammar books. In modern English grammar they are determiners. See Lesson 2!

Lesson 2

👉 Determiners = the Pointer

👆
Determiners point to the noun and say which one, whose one, how much, or how many (point, own, and count).
They come first, before any adjective.
thedetermineroldadjectivebooknoun
mydeterminerbigadjectivedognoun

Order: 👆 Pointer first → 🎨 Paint next → 📦 Noun last

👆 Tap a card to flip it and see the words.

👆

P = Point

answers "which one?"
tap to flip

👆 P = Point answers "which one?"

  • Demonstratives: this, that, these, those
  • Article: the
  • Interrogatives (question words): which, what (which book? what color?)
🎁

O = Own

answers "whose one?"
tap to flip

🎁 O = Own answers "whose one?"

  • Possessives (owners): my, your, his, her, its, our, their
  • Interrogative (question word): whose (whose bag?)
🔢

C = Count

answers "how many?" or "how much?"
tap to flip

🔢 C = Count answers "how many?" or "how much?"

  • Numbers: one, two, three (and first, second, third for order)
  • Article: a, an (means "one")
  • Quantifiers: countable (how many): many, few, several, both, a couple of
  • Quantifiers: uncountable (how much): much, little, a little
  • Quantifiers: work with both: some, any, no, all, more, most, enough
  • Distributives: one at a time: each, every, either, neither
🧠
Quick reminders
• a / an sit under Count because they mean "one". the sits under Point because it points to a special noun.
• Point and Own never go together: the my book ❌. Count can follow them: my two cats ✅
• whose and which keep their group: whose = Own, which = Point.

Lesson 3

🦸 Helping Verbs = the Sidekicks

🦸
A helping verb is the SIDEKICK of the main verb.
The hero (main verb) does the action. The sidekick adds time or power.
Shesubjectishelperreadingmain verb
Isubjectcanhelperswimmain verb

Team 1: The Primary Trio (be · have · do)

be
am · is · are · was · were
"is running" (happening now)
have
have · has · had
"have finished" (done!)
do
do · does · did
"Do you like it?" / "I did not go"

They have special powers, never change shape, and the next verb stays in its simple form.
